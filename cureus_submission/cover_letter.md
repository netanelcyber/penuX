I am submitting the manuscript titled **"PenuX: A Comparative Study of 11 Machine Learning and Deep Learning Models for Early Severity Prediction of Acute Pancreatitis Using Routine Admission Laboratory Values, with FHIR R4 Integration"** for consideration as an original research article.

**Clinical problem addressed:**
Severe Acute Pancreatitis (SAP) carries 20–30% mortality, yet the established scoring systems (Ranson, BISAP, APACHE II) require 24–48 hours of serial observation — a dangerous delay for the sickest patients. This study demonstrates that routine admission blood tests, processed by machine learning, can replace this wait with an admission-time prediction.

**Key contributions:**
1. The largest comparative evaluation of ML architectures for SAP severity prediction — 11 models across classical ML, MLP deep learning, and 5 LSTM-based sequence models — on a 106-feature cohort (n=722)
2. Identification of a label inversion effect (mild biliary AP cases showing higher WBC/CRP/lipase than severe necrotising AP) — a clinically important cohort insight with implications for IPN misclassification
3. A fully open-source platform (PenuX) with FHIR R4, HL7 v2.x, and Israeli HIS (Camelion) integration — available at https://penux.uk and https://github.com/netanelcyber/penuX
