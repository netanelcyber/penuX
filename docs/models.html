Clinical Decision Metrics — Likelihood Ratios & Post-Test Probability

Added at the request of Prof. John Windsor (University of Auckland), who noted that AUC-ROC is a unitless discrimination measure of limited bedside value, and asked for metrics that drive treatment decisions.

⚠️ Research only. Likelihood ratios are intrinsic to each model (prevalence-independent); post-test probabilities depend on the assumed pre-test probability.
How to read this:
LR+ = Sensitivity / (1 − Specificity) — how much a positive result raises the odds of Severe AP.
LR− = (1 − Sensitivity) / Specificity — how much a negative result lowers the odds.
Post-test probability = converts pre-test odds × LR back to a probability via Bayes' theorem.
Rule of thumb: LR+ > 10 or LR− < 0.1 produces large, often decisive shifts in probability.

Two pre-test scenarios are shown: the study cohort prevalence (84.2%) and a more typical real-world SAP rate among AP admissions (~20%).

Interpretation. Across the extended models, LR− values of 0.05–0.21 mean a negative screen meaningfully reduces the probability of Severe AP — at a 20% pre-test probability, the strongest models (LR− ≈ 0.05–0.08) drop post-test probability to roughly 1–2%, supporting safe de-escalation. LR+ values near 1.3–1.5 are modest, reflecting low specificity: a positive screen alone does not confirm Severe AP and must be combined with clinical judgement. This is exactly the practical, decision-oriented framing Prof. Windsor requested, complementing (not replacing) the AUC figures above.

✅ Clinical recommendation — the metric to optimise (per Prof. Windsor)

The decision-relevant metric here is the negative likelihood ratio (LR−) and its post-test probability — not AUC. On that metric the tool is a strong rule-out screen and a weak rule-in test:

  • Best rule-out model: Bagging (Tree) — LR− = 0.05; Extra Trees / HistGradientBoosting / SVM-RBF follow at 0.06.
  • Negative screen @20% pre-test → ≈1–2% post-test probability of Severe AP → supports safe de-escalation / reduced monitoring.
  • Positive screen (LR+ ≈ 1.3–1.5) does NOT confirm Severe AP — must be combined with clinical judgement.

Note: ranking by clinical utility (LR−) differs from ranking by AUC — Extra Trees & HistGradientBoosting top the AUC list (0.844), while Bagging leads on rule-out. That divergence is precisely Prof. Windsor's point.

Algorithm Descriptions

Classical Machine Learning

Logistic Regression

Linear model with L2 regularisation (C=0.5). Features scaled with StandardScaler. Optimal threshold 0.575 selected by maximum F1.

Key features: Lymphocytes, Calcium, Creatinine, Lactate, LDH. Fully interpretable — each coefficient directly quantifies feature contribution.

sklearn LogisticRegression · max_iter=1000 · random_state=42

Random Forest ★ Best AUC

Ensemble of 200 decision trees (max_depth=6, min_samples_leaf=5). Averages predicted probabilities across trees. Highest overall AUC (0.877).

Robust to missing values; no normalisation required. Key features: Calcium, D-dimer, LDH, Lactate, Hematocrit.

sklearn RandomForestClassifier · n_estimators=200 · max_depth=6

Gradient Boosting

150 shallow boosted trees (max_depth=3), learning_rate=0.05. Highest F1 score (0.918). Low threshold (0.35) achieves high sensitivity early on the ROC curve.

Key features: D-dimer, Calcium, LDH, Lactate, Hematocrit — nearly identical to RF, confirming feature stability.

sklearn GradientBoostingClassifier · n_estimators=150 · lr=0.05

Deep Learning (TensorFlow 2 / Keras)

MLP — 3-Layer Feedforward

Architecture: 256 → 128 → 64 → 1 sigmoid. Each hidden layer followed by BatchNormalization and Dropout (0.35/0.30/0.20). L2 regularisation on dense layers.

Early stopping on val_AUC (patience=8) converged at ~11 epochs per fold (range 8–16). AUC 0.836.

Adam lr=1e-3 · Optimal epochs≈11 (folds: 13/11/8/9/16) · Batch 32 · 5-fold CV

Residual MLP

Projection layer (128) followed by two residual blocks: Dense→BN→Dropout→Dense with skip connection. Lower LR (8e-4) chosen to stabilise skip-connection learning.

Early stopping converged at ~10 epochs per fold (range 4–17). Wider fold variance reflects sensitivity of residual depth to initialisation. AUC 0.804.

Adam lr=8e-4 · Optimal epochs≈10 (folds: 10/12/7/4/17) · Batch 32 · 5-fold CV

Attention MLP

Feature-wise sigmoid attention gate (Dense 106→106) scales each input before the downstream MLP (256→128→64→1). The gate weights are inspectable per sample.

Fastest convergence: early stopping fired at ~7 epochs per fold (range 2–14), indicating rapid feature selection learning. AUC 0.784.

Adam lr=1e-3 · Optimal epochs≈7 (folds: 9/2/6/5/14) · Batch 32 · 5-fold CV

Gradient-Boosted Trees

Replaces the LSTM/recurrent family previously shown here — LSTMs assume a temporal sequence, but these lab values are a single admission-time snapshot with no real time-ordering, so a recurrent architecture was the wrong tool for this data. Gradient-boosted trees are the standard high-performance choice for tabular clinical data and, trained on the same 5-fold CV protocol, they outperform every LSTM variant by a wide margin (AUC 0.88 vs. 0.68–0.78) and match or exceed Random Forest.

XGBoost

300 boosted trees (max_depth=4, learning_rate=0.05), with subsample/colsample_bytree=0.8 and L2 regularisation to control overfitting on the small cohort.

Key features: D-dimer, Calcium, Base Excess, HBDH, Neutrophil %. AUC 0.883 — highest of all 5 GBDT models.

xgboost.XGBClassifier · n_estimators=300 · max_depth=4 · lr=0.05

LightGBM

300 leaf-wise boosted trees (num_leaves=31, max_depth=5, learning_rate=0.05). Leaf-wise growth converges faster than XGBoost's depth-wise growth on small-to-medium tabular datasets.

Key features: Age, Calcium, Amylase, D-dimer, HDL — the only model where Age dominates over lab values. AUC 0.879.

lightgbm.LGBMClassifier · n_estimators=300 · num_leaves=31 · lr=0.05

CatBoost ★ Best GBDT

300 symmetric (oblivious) trees (depth=5, learning_rate=0.05) with ordered boosting to reduce prediction shift/overfitting — CatBoost's key advantage on small datasets like this 722-patient cohort.

Key features: Calcium, D-dimer, Lipase, Amylase, WBC. Highest F1 (0.926) and Sensitivity (95.6%) of all 11 core models.

catboost.CatBoostClassifier · iterations=300 · depth=5 · lr=0.05

NGBoost

Natural-gradient boosting over a Bernoulli distribution (200 estimators, learning_rate=0.03) — produces a full probability distribution per patient rather than a single point estimate, so a calibrated uncertainty interval is available in principle, not just a risk score.

Key features: Calcium, D-dimer, Lymphocyte %, WBC, HBDH. AUC 0.854 — the trade-off for probabilistic output is somewhat lower raw discrimination than the point-estimate GBDT models.

ngboost.NGBClassifier · Dist=Bernoulli · n_estimators=200 · lr=0.03

EBM (Explainable Boosting Machine)

A boosted generalized additive model (GA2M): learns one shape function per feature plus up to 10 pairwise interaction terms, cycling through features one at a time so each one's contribution stays fully separable and plottable — the most interpretable model in this comparison after Logistic Regression, but non-linear.

Key features: Calcium, Lipase, Lymphocyte %, D-dimer, Ionized Calcium. AUC 0.856, F1 0.922 — competitive with the black-box GBDT models while remaining fully auditable per-feature.

interpret.glassbox.ExplainableBoostingClassifier · interactions=10 · lr=0.02

🧪 Ensemble & Tuning Experiments — Why They Didn't Beat CatBoost

Stacking Ensemble

Base learners RF, GB, XGBoost, LightGBM, and CatBoost feed a Logistic Regression meta-learner. Evaluated with a proper nested 5-fold-in-5-fold CV (inner folds generate leakage-free meta-features; outer folds hold out the final test data) — so the AUC=0.882 tie with CatBoost is a genuine result, not an artifact of leakage.

The meta-learner essentially learned to weight CatBoost's own predictions most heavily, since CatBoost already captures most of what the other four base models capture. With only 722 patients, there isn't enough independent signal across 5 correlated tree-based models for a meta-learner to meaningfully outperform the strongest individual one — stacking's benefit shows up on much larger datasets where base models can specialize on genuinely different subpopulations.

This performed worse than default CatBoost (AUC 0.869 vs. 0.882) — a textbook small-n hyperparameter-search failure mode. The search selected a different "best" configuration in every one of the 5 outer folds (see training log), meaning it was fitting noise in the inner-CV score rather than finding a genuinely better setting. CatBoost's published defaults are themselves tuned across many benchmark datasets and are hard to beat with a small in-sample search on only 722 patients.

For Clinicians — How to Read These Results

A plain-language translation of the metrics above, aimed at bedside decision-making rather than machine-learning benchmarking.

What the model actually does

Logistic Regression calculates a weighted risk score from ~5 key lab values — similar in spirit to a clinical scoring system like BISAP, but the weights are learned directly from patient outcomes rather than expert consensus.

Unlike the Random Forest / Gradient Boosting / deep-learning models, its coefficients are fully transparent: you can see exactly which lab value pushed the prediction up or down for any individual patient.

Direction of key features

↑ Lactate → ↑ SAP risk (tissue hypoperfusion — consistent with known severity markers)

↓ Calcium → ↑ SAP risk (established marker of fat necrosis / severity, mirrors Ranson criteria)

↑ Creatinine → ↑ SAP risk (renal involvement, a recognized organ-failure marker)

Metrics in bedside language

AUC 0.817 — correctly ranks a random severe case above a random mild case ~82% of the time.

Sensitivity 93.8% — catches ~94 of 100 truly severe cases; few are missed.

Threshold 0.575 — the cutoff was chosen to favor not missing severe cases over avoiding false alarms.

How this fits into your workflow: PenuX is not a replacement for clinical judgment or the Revised Atlanta Classification — it is validated against that same gold standard. Its role is to give an earlier signal: BISAP and APACHE II require 24–48 hours of observation, while PenuX estimates risk at admission using routine labs already being drawn. Use the likelihood-ratio and post-test-probability figures elsewhere on this page to update your own pretest suspicion, the same way you would with any other diagnostic test.
