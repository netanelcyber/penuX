Research prototype · acute pancreatitis

Early prediction of deterioration before severe disease is fully observable.

PenuX-AP-Severity studies whether early laboratory and clinical data can identify patients who will later meet criteria for severe acute pancreatitis. XGBoost is the principal machine-learning benchmark, while Revised Atlanta remains the outcome definition used to label severity.

Explanation.

Revised Atlanta is not a competing prediction algorithm. It is the reference definition used to determine whether the patient ultimately had mild, moderately severe, or severe acute pancreatitis. PenuX attempts to estimate that future outcome earlier, when only admission or first-24-hour information is available.

Therefore the scientifically correct question is not “Is XGBoost more accurate than Atlanta?” but “How accurately can XGBoost predict a later Atlanta-defined severe outcome before that outcome is fully known?”

How to interpret these findings

1. Discrimination

AUROC answers whether higher-risk patients tend to receive higher model scores than lower-risk patients. A value around 0.92 is strong discrimination, but it does not determine the best clinical/research threshold.

2. High-sensitivity operation

PenuX targets ≥98% sensitivity for a watch threshold. This changes the problem from “maximize overall classification accuracy” to “miss as few future SAP cases as possible while preserving useful specificity.”

3. Calibration

If a model outputs a risk of 0.30, calibration asks whether approximately 30% of comparable patients actually experience the outcome. This matters if probabilities are displayed to researchers or used in decision-curve analysis.

4. External validation

A model that performs well in one hospital may degrade elsewhere because laboratory practices, case mix and prevalence differ. Independent-site validation is therefore more important than simply adding more internal folds.

Validation workflow

Development

Stratified development/test separation. Hyperparameters and model identity must be determined without using final-test outcomes.

Out-of-fold prediction

OOF probabilities are used to compare candidate models and lock the high-sensitivity operating threshold.

Final test

The selected model is refit on development data and evaluated once on the held-out test set, with bootstrap intervals and a documented manifest.

Success criterion for the research program.

The most informative result is not simply AUROC > 0.90. The central question is whether the model can maintain ≥98% sensitivity while retaining clinically meaningful specificity and calibration, and whether that operating point transports to an independent institution.

Primary reporting target: Sensitivity ≥ 0.98 + specificity at the locked threshold + NPV / PPV + AUROC / AUPRC with confidence intervals + calibration + independent external validation
