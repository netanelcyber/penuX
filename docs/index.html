Research prototype · acute pancreatitis

Computational early-warning model for future deterioration.

PenuX-AP-Severity studies whether early laboratory and clinical data can identify patients who will later meet criteria for severe acute pancreatitis. XGBoost is the principal machine-learning engine, while Revised Atlanta remains the reference outcome definition.

Computational model

For each eligible patient, the model receives an early feature vector containing laboratory measurements and selected demographics available at prediction time.

X = [WBC, CRP, BUN, Creatinine, Glucose, Calcium, Albumin, LDH, Amylase, Hb, APTT, PT, ...]

1. XGBoost ensemble score

XGBoost combines many regularized decision trees. Each tree contributes a small correction to the patient's risk score.

S(X) = Σm=1M η · fm(X)

fm(X) is the output of tree m, η is the learning rate, and M is the number of boosting rounds.

2. Probability transformation

The accumulated score is transformed into an estimated probability of the future severe outcome.

P(SAP | X) = 1 / (1 + e−S(X))

When probability calibration is enabled, the raw model probability is recalibrated using development-only data before interpretation.

Outcome definition.

The target is normalized as 0 = non-SAP and 1 = SAP. Revised Atlanta supplies the reference severity definition. The machine-learning task is to predict that later outcome earlier, not to replace the Atlanta definition itself.

Worked computational example

Example inputs — illustrative only WBC = 18.4 CRP = 212 BUN = 10.8 Creatinine = 1.42 Glucose = 196 Calcium = 1.91 Albumin = 29 LDH = 510 ... XGBoost raw score S(X) = 2.11 sigmoid(2.11) = 0.892 estimated SAP risk = 89.2%

The numbers above demonstrate the computation only. They are not a validated patient example or a PenuX clinical threshold.

High-sensitivity alert equation

The watch threshold is learned and locked from development out-of-fold predictions, rather than assuming a generic probability cutoff of 0.50.

Alert(X) = 1 if P(SAP|X) ≥ Twatch, otherwise 0

The research objective is to choose Twatch so development sensitivity is at least 98%, then apply that fixed threshold to held-out or external data.

Internal Medicine Ward use case

Patient state

Hospitalized with acute pancreatitis in an internal medicine ward and not already known to have the future endpoint at prediction time.

Prediction window

Admission and first-24-hour laboratory data are the primary static-cohort window. Longitudinal cohorts may support 6 h, 12 h and 24 h forecasts.

Research question

Does the current laboratory pattern contain information associated with subsequent Atlanta-defined severe acute pancreatitis or deterioration?

No outcome leakage.

Variables that directly encode the future outcome, post-deterioration interventions, post-baseline events, or severity information unavailable at prediction time must be excluded from the early-warning feature set.

Credentialed or multi-center EHR sources can be used for transportability and longitudinal deterioration analyses when their diagnosis, laboratory and outcome definitions can be harmonized. Restricted patient-level files are never committed to this repository.

Validation workflow

Development

Split by patient/cohort as appropriate. Tune XGBoost and any feature-selection or class-weight parameters without access to final-test outcomes.

Out-of-fold prediction

Generate OOF probabilities to compare configurations, calibrate risk and lock the high-sensitivity threshold.

Held-out / external test

Apply the frozen model and frozen threshold once, reporting AUROC, AUPRC, sensitivity, specificity, PPV, NPV, calibration and uncertainty.

Multi-cohort validation.

The preferred extension is leave-one-cohort-out evaluation: train on eligible source cohorts and evaluate on a hospital/cohort not used for fitting. Cohort identity should be used for splitting and reporting, not as a shortcut predictor of severity.
